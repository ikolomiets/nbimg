# Peter Greenaway

## Essence

Greenaway approaches cinema as painting, architecture, catalog, and performance at once. Bodies, food, text, furniture, costume, and rooms are arranged into images dense with pattern, symmetry, art history, sensuality, and decay. Formal beauty is knowingly artificial and often darkly comic; systems of counting, classification, or repetition promise order while human appetites overwhelm them.

## Using the style flexibly

Give the scene a strong organizing idea—a spatial pattern, visual sequence, family of textures, or relationship between bodies and objects—then allow irregular life to press against it. The image may be sparse or abundant, contemporary or ancient, bright or shadowed. Avoid copying banquet tables, Dutch paintings, calligraphy on skin, or piling on baroque décor without an underlying structure and tension.

**Representative works:** *The Draughtsman's Contract*; *A Zed & Two Noughts*; *The Cook, the Thief, His Wife & Her Lover*; *Prospero's Books*; *The Pillow Book*.
