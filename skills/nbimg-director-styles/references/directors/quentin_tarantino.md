# Quentin Tarantino

## Essence

Tarantino treats genre history as a vivid present tense. People occupy diners, cars, rooms, streets, and landscapes with the self-awareness of performers entering a charged scene. Framing makes conversation, costume, posture, and small objects feel consequential, while tension can stretch patiently before breaking into graphic action. The look is playful, confrontational, and deeply informed by older cinema.

## Using the style flexibly

Identify the scene's genre energy and heighten it through confident staging, expressive detail, and a clear shift between anticipation and release. Any period or culture can work if it remains specific to the user's subject rather than becoming a collage of borrowed clichés. Use visual quotation as a broad sensibility, not reproduction. Avoid copied costumes, trunk shots, bare feet, yellow jumpsuits, vintage signage, or violence as the only stylistic cue.

**Representative works:** *Reservoir Dogs*; *Pulp Fiction*; *Kill Bill*; *Inglourious Basterds*; *Once Upon a Time in Hollywood*.
