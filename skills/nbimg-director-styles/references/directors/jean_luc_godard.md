# Jean-Luc Godard

## Essence

Godard treats an image as something to question, interrupt, and rediscover. Documentary immediacy can collide with bold color, written language, posed performance, fragments of advertising, art, politics, or popular genre. A scene may feel casual and rigorously composed at once, reminding the viewer that cinema is both a window onto life and a constructed arrangement of sound, image, and ideas.

## Using the style flexibly

Let the requested scene keep its identity while introducing one lucid disruption: an unexpected relationship between foreground and background, a graphic block of color, a direct gaze, a meaningful reflection, or a fragmentary visual rhythm. The result can be elegant, rough, monochrome, digital, romantic, or analytical. Avoid copying jump cuts in still-image form, adding arbitrary French text, or reducing the style to 1960s fashion and primary colors.

**Representative works:** *Breathless*; *Contempt*; *Pierrot le Fou*; *Every Man for Himself*; *Histoire(s) du cinéma*.
